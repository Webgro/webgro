01

Two separate things have happened

Telling them apart matters, because only one of them is still costing you money.

Estimated organic sessions Ranking keywords

Semrush UK database, monthly snapshots on a consistent methodology since 2022. These are modelled estimates and will not match your analytics exactly, though the shape of the trend is dependable.

Organic traffic reached an estimated peak of 2,585 sessions in July 2025 and had fallen to 641 by that December, losing roughly three quarters of its volume across five months while the site was still running on WordPress. That period accounts for the bulk of the damage, and it happened well before the replatform, which means the cause sits in how the pages themselves were built and organised rather than in anything that changed in February.

The migration to Shopify then removed a large number of legacy URLs, and that is what produces the steep fall in ranking keyword count between February and May 2026. Those pages were, for the most part, earning very little, which is why the traffic line moves far less than the keyword line across the same window. Clearing out low value pages during a replatform is normal practice and the keyword count reads worse than the commercial reality behind it.

The migration explains the keyword count. It does not explain the traffic. The recoverable ground is in the 2025 decline, and that is where the work below is aimed.

02

Nine pages competing to be the same page

Whichever way the traffic history is read, this is the problem sitting underneath it.

Critical

A test page is live, indexable, and 99.2% identical to your main commercial page

The URL /pages/duplicate-of-glass-balustrades-for-testing returns a 200 status, carries its own self-referencing canonical tag, appears in your XML sitemap, and shares an identical title tag and H1 heading with /pages/glass-balustrades. We measured the body text overlap between the two at 99.2 per cent, which leaves Google holding two effectively identical documents and being asked to work out which of them should rank for your most commercially important term.

Sitting alongside it, /pages/test-iframe is also live and indexable with 34 words of content on it.

Critical

Seven landing pages built from a single template

Garden, patio, outdoor, decking, indoor, balcony and stairs each have a dedicated page under /pages/, and the word counts across all seven land between 3,459 and 3,483. Text overlap against the main balustrade page runs between 92.4 and 93.3 per cent, while garden measured against patio comes out at 94.8 per cent, which tells you they were produced from one template with the modifier swapped rather than written as pages in their own right.

The search intent behind garden, patio and outdoor glass balustrades is close enough to identical that Google returns broadly the same results for all three, so what could have been one authoritative page has instead been divided into several weak ones, and Google's guidance on scaled content abuse addresses this pattern directly.

Critical

The same topic covered twice, under /pages/ and again under /collections/

You are running both /pages/juliet-balconies and /collections/juliet-balconies, and the same duplication exists between /pages/glass-balustrade-stairs and /collections/staircase-balustrades. Semrush attributes 28 keywords to each of the two Juliet URLs, which is the signature of Google alternating between them rather than settling on one, and neither URL accumulates the authority it would need to compete properly.

For comparison, The Glass Warehouse holds first place for "juliet balcony" at 8,100 searches a month using a single URL. You sit at thirteenth using two.

03

What the content programme has produced

There are 236 blog posts on the site. We checked every one of them against the UK search index, and 209 rank for nothing whatsoever. Five of them account for all the blog traffic the site receives.

Critical

Fifty nine location posts for towns you have no presence in

The blog carries 59 posts following the pattern "Glass Balustrade in Lisburn", "Glass Balustrade in Bingley", "Glass Balustrade in Chorley", "Glass Balustrade in Twickenham" and so on through Wigan, Poole, Wakefield, Preston, Oldham, Taunton, Southall, Kensington and roughly fifty more. The median length is 370 words and 38 of the 59 fall under 400 words.

Three of those 59 posts appear anywhere in the UK index. To be fair to the work, these were individually written rather than mechanically spun, and we measured the text overlap between them at only 7 to 9 per cent, so real effort went into producing them. The difficulty is strategic. You are a national supplier shipping components across the country, you hold no premises in Bingley or Taunton, and Google has no reason to treat you as a local result in those places. The budget was spent on a strategy that could not have worked.

High

The blog competes against itself on the questions that matter

Several of your highest intent topics have been written multiple times, so the posts split the signal between them in exactly the way the landing pages do. Cost is covered by "How much does a glass balustrade cost?", "How much is a Glass Balustrade per meter?", "The Cost-Effectiveness Of Glass Balustrades" and "Is a Glass Balustrade Expensive?". Height and regulations are covered by "What height should a glass balustrade be?", "What is the Legal Height for a Glass Balustrade?", "Balustrade Regulations UK", "A Complete Guide to Glass Balustrade Regulations in the UK" and "Understanding UK Building Regulations for Glass Balustrades in 2026". Installation is covered by both "How to install a glass balustrade" and "How to fit a glass balustrade".

Our clustering found 18 groups of near duplicate topics covering 115 of the 236 posts. Seven pairs share an exactly identical title, including two posts both called "Glass Balustrade", two called "glass balustrade blog" and two asking whether you need planning permission for a Juliet balcony.

High

The library is too thin to compete on the terms it targets

The median blog post runs to 399 words, 119 of the 236 fall under 400 words, and 218 of them fall under 700. Cost, regulations and specification questions are exactly where a supplier can win authority in this category, and they are also the questions where the competing results are thorough guides rather than short posts.

Worth noting that the raw material is here. Your guide to UK glass balustrade loading requirements already picks up 7 keywords including "bs 6180:2011", which shows the technical angle works when it is given proper depth.

The scale of it

Across the whole site, 1,168 URLs are indexed and 112 of them rank for a single keyword or more. Twenty two generate any traffic. Put another way, more than nine in ten pages on the site are doing nothing at all in search, and a substantial share of them were created deliberately as part of a content programme.

09

Buyers are starting to ask assistants instead of Google

Choosing balustrade components is a specification matching problem, which happens to be exactly the kind of question people now put to ChatGPT and similar tools rather than to a search box.

Somebody working out which clamp suits 13.5mm glass on a 48mm post, or whether a particular spigot meets the loading requirement for a domestic landing, is asking a question with a single correct answer that depends on matching several numbers together. Assistants handle that far better than a list of blue links does, and the people asking are frequently installers and specifiers rather than browsers, which makes them the enquiries you most want to win. Your catalogue of 738 components is genuinely well suited to being the source those answers get drawn from, provided the information is presented in a form a machine can read with confidence.

Already right

Nothing is blocking the AI crawlers

Your robots.txt declares rules for only two agents, the wildcard and adsbot-google, which means GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended and the rest all have unrestricted access to the site today. A surprising number of Shopify stores have quietly blocked these, so there is nothing to undo before the rest of the work can start.

High

Your agent instructions file describes nothing about your products

Shopify publishes an /agents.md file for your store and lists it in your sitemap, so shopping agents are being pointed at it. The version currently live is Shopify's managed default, which directs agents towards Shopify's own purchasing tools and tells them nothing whatsoever about what you sell, which glass thicknesses you serve, how your systems differ from one another, or how somebody should choose between them.

It can be replaced. Adding an agents.md.liquid template to the theme overrides the managed file, and because the same template is the fallback for /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, one piece of work covers all three agent discovery URLs. The trade off is that a custom file stops updating itself in step with your commerce configuration, so somebody has to keep it accurate as ranges change, which is precisely the sort of thing that gets written once and then quietly goes stale.

That file is the closest thing you have to a briefing document for every AI agent that visits, and at present it is doing none of that work.

High

Your specifications are readable by people and invisible to machines

On the component pages we examined, glass thickness, material, finish, fixing type and load information appear in prose and inside a single HTML table, while the Product structured data carries no additionalProperty entries at all, which is the field designed to hold exactly this information in a form a machine can parse without guessing.

Specifications written only into sentences are the first thing lost when a model condenses a page, so a component that is perfectly well described for a human reader can still fail to surface when an assistant is matching a 13.5mm panel to a compatible clamp. The 95 products currently sharing a duplicate title compound this, because an assistant asked to distinguish between them has nothing to distinguish them by.

How we would measure it

Worth being straight about this, because a good deal of what is currently sold as AI optimisation is difficult to hold anyone to. There is no dependable rank tracker for assistant answers in the way there is for Google positions, so we would measure it through referral traffic arriving from assistant sources in GA4, through crawler activity in your server logs, and through running a fixed set of buying questions against the major assistants each month and recording whether you are cited. That gives you a directional read rather than a precise one, and we would rather set that expectation now than report against a number that cannot be verified.

What we would do, and in what order

Sequenced by impact and by what has to happen before something else can work. The first two months are deliberately unglamorous.

Months 1 to 2 · Remediation

Remove the duplication and recover what the migration dropped

  • Remove the two test pages and clear them from the XML sitemap
  • Merge garden, patio and indoor into their parent pages with 301 redirects in place
  • Resolve the /pages/ against /collections/ conflicts so that one canonical URL owns each topic, redirecting the other
  • Consolidate the duplicated blog topics on cost, height, regulations and installation into one authoritative post each, redirecting the rest
  • Decide the fate of the 59 location posts, consolidating the handful with any traction and retiring the remainder
  • Audit the February migration redirects and recover any URLs that were ranking and were retired without a redirect pointing somewhere sensible
  • Establish a Search Console and Analytics baseline so that everything after this can be measured against it
Months 3 to 6 · Rebuild

Make the pages that survive worth ranking

  • Rewrite the Juliet balcony and glass balustrade categories as proper buying pages, covering system comparison, glass specification, loading requirements under BS 6180:2011, and lead times
  • Rewrite decking, frameless, stairs and balcony as four genuinely distinct pages rather than one template
  • Clear the duplicate product titles and descriptions, starting with the 38 groups where multiple items share a name
  • Rewrite the over-length product titles so the distinguishing detail survives into the search result
  • Build a custom agents.md.liquid template so the file briefs AI agents properly on your ranges, glass thicknesses and system differences, covering /agents.md, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt in one go
  • Add structured specification data to product pages, covering glass thickness, fixing type, material, finish and load rating
  • Start the monthly article programme, aimed at the specification questions your customers ask before they buy
Month 6 onward · Authority and reach

Close the referring domain gap and widen where you appear

  • Sustained link acquisition through trade and specification placements, built up steadily rather than in bursts, which also feeds AI visibility because assistants lean on sources they see corroborated elsewhere
  • Extend the structured specification work across the remainder of the 738 product catalogue
  • Build the compatibility and selection content that answers "which component do I need" directly, in the format assistants quote from
  • Move on the head terms you are absent from, starting with "balustrade" at 12,100 searches a month
  • Restructure internal linking so that authority reaches the pages that carry commercial value
  • Content aimed at architects, builders and self builders, where the enquiry values are highest
